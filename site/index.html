How it works

  1. 01

    Point it at your folders

    Each genre folder you already keep becomes a group. No folders yet? It can break an unsorted pile into candidate groups for you to audition, name and keep.

  2. 02

    Sort new music

    Every new track gets its likely groups, each with a score. Listen to it on its waveform and press 1–5 to pick, or s to skip.

  3. 03

    File it, or undo it

    See exactly what will be written before anything is. Then copy, move, link or just make playlists. Changed your mind? Undo puts everything back.

What it does

Learns your folders, not a generic genre list

Your folders are the training examples. The groups are yours, and it never invents a category on its own.

Tells you how sure it is

Every suggestion has a confidence score. A track that sounds like nothing you own is reported as a new sound, not forced into the closest folder.

Checks your folders

See how well your groups can be told apart, and which two folders overlap most. That tells you something about your library, too.

Auto-tags every track

Genre, beat type (four to the floor, half-time, 2-step, breaks), mood, energy, danceability and key with its Camelot code. Each tag is a readable rule, so you can see why a track got it.

Sorts samples too

Kicks, snares, claps, hats, basses and chords. One-shots are measured as single sounds, by attack, decay and where their energy sits.

Exports to your DJ app

Every group as a playlist for Rekordbox, Traktor, Serato, Engine DJ or VirtualDJ, with BPM, key and genre.

Screenshots

Your files stay where they are until you say so

Analysing, sorting and reviewing only write to ${name}'s own database. Your music is touched only when you commit a session, after a preview of exactly what will happen.

  • Dry run first. Every filing mode shows its plan before it runs.
  • Undo afterwards. One command or click puts every file back.
  • Four filing modes. Playlists only, copy, move, or link. Playlist mode never moves a file.
  • Your audio never leaves your computer. No account and no tracking.

Questions

Will it move or rename my files?

Not unless you commit a session, and not before it has shown you every file it will write. Every commit can be undone. Playlist mode and DJ app exports only write new playlist files; your music stays put.

Does it upload my music anywhere?

No. Analysis runs on your computer and the library is a file in your home folder. There is one optional extra, off by default: an AI model can suggest names for discovered groups. If you turn it on, only track metadata (artist, title, genre, tempo) is sent, never audio.

How many folders and tracks do I need?

Two folders is the minimum. A dozen or more tracks in each gives the best results, and it keeps learning from every track you file.

I use Rekordbox. Will this break my library?

Exports write a Rekordbox XML file you import, so your collection stays as it is. If your DJ app already manages a track, use playlist or copy filing: moving a file breaks the app's link to it and loses its cue points.

How is it different from tagging by genre?

Genre tags are someone else's opinion. ${name} sorts into your folders, by how the tracks in them actually sound, so a folder called "Sunday" works as well as one called "Deep House".

macOS says the app is from an unidentified developer

The builds are not signed yet. Right-click the app and choose Open the first time; on Windows, choose "More info", then "Run anyway".
